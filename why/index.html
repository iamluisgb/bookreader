What you read should stay with you.

You finish a good technical book. A month later you remember the cover and two ideas. That is the problem BookReader is for, and these are the rules it follows.

1. Understand it, don’t skip it.

When a paragraph doesn’t click, many reading tools offer to summarise the chapter for you. That saves the reading and loses the book.

BookReader does the opposite. You ask about the part you don’t get, in your own words, and the agent explains it at your level, using the whole book and your reading goal. Then you go back to the page and keep reading.

2. Make nothing up.

An explanation you can’t check is worth little. Every answer comes from passages of the book and carries its citation. One tap and you are on the page, in the sentence that says it.

If the book doesn’t cover something, the agent says so. It doesn’t fill the gap with something that sounds right.

3. Remembering takes a little effort.

Reading something twice feels like learning. Trying to recall it is what makes it stay. So BookReader asks you things: flashcards that come back right before you would forget, a question when you finish a chapter, and Feynman mode, where you explain the idea and the book checks you.

Every card remembers the page it came from, so going back to the book is one tap away.

4. Your books are yours.

No account. Your books, notes and cards live on your device, and you can export everything whenever you want. The agent uses your own AI key with the provider you choose, and your questions go straight from your browser to that provider.

The details, including exactly what does leave your device, are on the privacy page.

5. What I won’t do.

Put ads next to your book. Sell or share what you read. Lock your notes in a format you can’t take with you.
