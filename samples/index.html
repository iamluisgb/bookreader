    How to read them

    1. Pick a book

    “Read it now” opens BookReader with that book already loaded. If you would rather keep the file, the link underneath downloads it: no DRM, no watermark.

    2. It stays in your library

    The book is kept on your device like any other you add yourself, so it is there next time and it opens without a connection.

    3. Read, highlight, ask

    The text is indexed on your device, so search, flashcards and the agent work offline. Nothing you do with these books is uploaded.

    Why these books, and what you may do with them

    All four are in the public domain, and each one is credited below. You can copy them, print them, quote them, put them on a USB stick and give them away.

    • Frankenstein (1818) and The Time Machine (1895) — editions by Standard Ebooks, whose editorial work is released under CC0. Covers by the same project. Mary Shelley died in 1851 and H. G. Wells in 1946.
    • Niebla (1914) — text from Project Gutenberg, distributed by Elejandría. Cover made for this page. Miguel de Unamuno died in 1936.
    • Bodas de sangre (1933) — text from Wikisource in Spanish, distributed by Elejandría. Cover made for this page. Federico García Lorca died in 1936.
    • Covers — Niebla: Morning Mist in the Mountains, Caspar David Friedrich (c. 1808); Bodas de sangre: Duelo a garrotazos, Francisco de Goya (1820-23). Both paintings are in the public domain (Wikimedia Commons); the cover design is ours.
    • The free library — classics from Standard Ebooks, which publishes works in the public domain in the United States and releases its own editorial work under CC0. Depending on your country, a few may still be under copyright there. Technical books: each one shows its licence and links to its source; the ones marked NC are offered here only because BookReader gives them away, never as part of a paid plan.

    The authors died more than 80 years ago, which is the term that applies in Spain to authors who died before 1987, and more than 70 in the rest of the European Union. The transcriptions come from volunteer projects; if you spot a typo, the fix belongs in the source, not here. Nothing on this page is scraped from a video, a course or a paid edition.
