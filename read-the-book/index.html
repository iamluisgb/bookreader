// 01 the problem

The problem.

You finish a technical book and you are sure you understood it. A month later you remember the cover and two ideas.

That is not a memory problem. Reading leaves nothing behind unless something pulls you back into the text. Highlights pile up in an app you never open again. Chatting with the PDF gives you an answer you cannot check, with no paragraph behind it. And a summary skips the reasoning entirely: you keep someone else's conclusion about a book you have not read.

// 04 what a summary costs you

What it costs you.

COMPREHENSION

A summary tells you where the author arrived. The book shows you why. The second one is what you can use on Monday.

RETENTION

Reading without recall afterwards is re-reading. Remembering takes effort: questions, not re-reads.

JUDGEMENT

If you never see the full argument you cannot disagree with it. You keep someone else's conclusion.

OWNERSHIP

Your library inside someone else's cloud is a subscription. Your files and notes are yours, and they leave with you.

MEMORY ACROSS BOOKS

What you learned in chapter 4 connects to a book from two years ago. That does not fit in a note card.

THE RIGHT TO BE BORED

The slow parts are where the idea settles in. Skipping them is an elegant way of not having read.
