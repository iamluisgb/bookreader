The problem.

You finish a technical book and you are sure you understood it. A month later you remember the cover and two ideas.

It is not a lack of memory, or of hours. Rereading, highlighting and reading a summary have one thing in common: everything feels familiar, and familiar is easy to mistake for known. What makes reading stick is the uncomfortable part: trying to recall it without looking, and trying again a few days later. Hardly anyone does it, because nothing asks them to.

The thesis

Understanding cannot be downloaded. If a book is worth eight hours, it is worth being asked about.

And why the whole book.

This part is not a study: it is what we believe. Being asked works with any text, but a summary leaves you very little to be asked about.

Comprehension

A summary tells you where the author arrived. The book shows you why. The second one is what you can use on Monday.

Judgement

If you never see the full argument you cannot disagree with it. You keep someone else’s conclusion.

Ownership

Your library inside someone else’s cloud is a subscription. Your files and notes are yours, and they leave with you.
